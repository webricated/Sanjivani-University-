Here are **clear, student-friendly notes on Behavioural Interviews and the STAR Technique**, suitable for engineering students preparing for placements and higher studies interviews.

---

## Behavioural Interviews

### What is a Behavioural Interview?

A **behavioural interview** focuses on how you handled situations in the past to predict how you will perform in the future.
Interviewers believe **past behaviour is the best indicator of future performance**.

Instead of:

* *“What would you do if…?”*

They ask:

* *“Tell me about a time when…”*

---

### Why Companies Use Behavioural Interviews

* To assess **problem-solving skills**
* To evaluate **teamwork and communication**
* To understand **leadership and initiative**
* To check **adaptability and stress management**
* To verify **real-world application of skills**

---

### Common Behavioural Interview Questions

* Tell me about a time you solved a difficult problem.
* Describe a situation where you worked in a team.
* Give an example of a failure and what you learned from it.
* Tell me about a time you handled conflict.
* Describe a time you showed leadership.

---

## STAR Technique

### What is the STAR Technique?

The **STAR Technique** is a **structured method** to answer behavioural interview questions clearly and effectively.

**STAR stands for:**

* **S – Situation**
* **T – Task**
* **A – Action**
* **R – Result**

---

### 1. Situation

Describe the **context or background**.

* Where did it happen?
* What was going on?

**Example:**
“During my final-year project, our team faced frequent bugs close to the submission deadline.”

---

### 2. Task

Explain your **responsibility or role**.

* What was expected from you?
* What problem needed to be solved?

**Example:**
“I was responsible for debugging and improving the performance of the application.”

---

### 3. Action

Describe **what you actually did**.

* Focus on *your* actions, not the team’s.
* Mention tools, techniques, or approaches used.

**Example:**
“I analyzed the code using debugging tools, optimized loops, and refactored inefficient data structures.”

---

### 4. Result

Share the **outcome**.

* What happened in the end?
* Quantify results if possible.
* Mention what you learned.

**Example:**
“As a result, the number of bugs reduced by 70%, and the project was submitted on time with positive feedback.”

---

## STAR Technique – Key Tips

* Be **specific**, not generic
* Keep answers **concise (1–2 minutes)**
* Use **real examples** (projects, internships, labs, hackathons)
* Highlight **skills relevant to the role**
* Avoid blaming others in failure examples

---

## Example STAR Answer (Technical)

**Question:** Tell me about a time you optimized a program.

**Answer:**

* **Situation:** In a lab assignment, our Java program was taking too long to execute for large inputs.
* **Task:** I needed to improve its performance.
* **Action:** I replaced nested loops with a HashMap-based approach and reduced time complexity from O(n²) to O(n).
* **Result:** The execution time improved significantly, and the solution was appreciated by the instructor.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

* Giving theoretical answers instead of real experiences
* Skipping the **Result**
* Speaking too long without structure
* Using “we” instead of “I” excessively

---

## Final Interview Tip

Think of **5–6 strong experiences** (projects, DSA problems, teamwork, leadership, failure) and **prepare STAR answers in advance**.
